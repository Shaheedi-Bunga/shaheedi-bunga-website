ਕੀਰਤਨ

Kirtan Vidyala

Kirtan, learned on the instruments it was written for: dilruba, taus, rabab and saranda.

ClassesNine a weekWhereGipsy LaneFeeNone

The discipline

A test of commitment.

None of it comes quickly. It asks real commitment and determination.

Gurmukhi is not needed to start, but it becomes necessary later, for shabads and Asa di Vaar.

Classes run nine times a week at Gipsy Lane: every weekday morning, two evenings, and both days of the weekend, and every one of them is open to any age. You do not join a term or wait for one to start; you come to the next class. There is no fee and no audition.

There are instruments to learn on, so you do not need your own to begin. You can get one later, once you know you will keep going.

The tanti saaj

Four instruments.

Strung, bowed or plucked, and played the way kirtan was played before the harmonium arrived.

ਦਿਲਰੁਬਾ

DilrubaBowed · fretted

A skin-covered body, frets along the neck, and a bank of sympathetic strings that ring under the melody.

ਤਾਊਸ

TausBowed · fretted

Larger and deeper than the dilruba, its body carved as a peacock. Taus is the Persian word for one.

ਰਬਾਬ

RababPlucked

A deep wooden bowl under a skin face: the saaj of Bhai Mardana Ji, who played as Guru Nanak Dev Ji sang.

ਸਰੰਦਾ

SarandaBowed · held upright

Its body is cut in at the waist, so the bow can reach each string on its own.

The vidyala

At the microphone.

Classes at Gipsy Lane, and the samagams the students play at once they are ready.
