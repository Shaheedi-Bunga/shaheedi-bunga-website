# How the site is written: voice C, clear charity (chosen 2026-10-28)

The user picked this voice from three drafts of the Classes page. Every page,
the homepage included, is written in it.

## The voice in one line

A registered charity telling people plainly what it does, where, when and for
whom: full sentences, the gurdwaras named, the facts first, warm without
trying to be.

## The reference: the Classes page, as approved

- **Heading:** Come and learn with us.
- **Opening:** Gurmat classes at gurdwaras in Leicester. Every one is free, and every teacher is a volunteer.
- **How it works:** Almost every class is open to all ages. There is no registration and no audition, and you take part from your first session.
- **Kirtan (the user's wording):** Learn kirtan on the instruments it was written for: dilruba, taus, rabab and saranda. (Then the facts: nine classes a week at Gurudwara Shri Guru Dashmesh Sahib, Gipsy Lane.)
- **Santhiya (the user's wording):** Learn to read Gurbani correctly: how each word is said, where to pause, and what it means. (Then the facts: every day at Gipsy Lane, and online.)
- **Your first week (the user's wording):**
  ੧ Send us a message. Tell us which class you'd like, or that you're not sure yet. One of the teachers will reply with this week's times.
  ੨ Come along. Arrive a few minutes early and cover your head. If you don't have an instrument, we'll lend you one, and granths are shared.
  ੩ Keep coming. You learn at your own pace, from people who learnt the same way.
- **Closing (the user's wording):** Not sure where to start? Santhiya is where every other class begins, because they all rest on reading Gurbani well. Send us a message and we'll help you choose.

The user mixed in these warmer lines (from the "warm and plain" draft) on purpose: voice C sets the structure and the facts, and it may use "we" and "you" this warmly.

## Do

- Lead with what the reader needs: what it is, when, where, who it is for, what it costs.
- Write full, plain sentences. "Use", not "utilise". Active voice.
- Name the gurdwaras: Guru Tegh Bahadur Gurdwara, 106 East Park Road; Gurudwara Shri Guru Dashmesh Sahib, 49 Gipsy Lane.
- Keep one or two memorable lines on a page, usually the heading. Every other line is plain.
- Keep Panjabi terms the sangat uses (santhiya, sangat, saaj, granth, samagam, vidyala), and make each one clear from its sentence.

## Don't (the habits the old text had)

- Slogan fragments and one-line closers: "Six ways in." "Four purposes, one practice." "Not a teacher, not a trustee."
- "No X, no Y" lists. State the absence once, plainly: "There is no registration and no audition."
- Contrast reveals ("It's not X, it's Y"), colon reveals ("The result: …") and self-answered questions. A question the reader would ask ("Not sure which class to choose?") is fine.
- Em dashes in headings and short lines. Exclamation marks. Inflated words: "unique", "journey", "transformative", "vibrant".
- Triads put in for rhythm.

## Never change

- **Facts.** Every time, day, address, age, number, fee and name stays exactly as it is. Change a fact only from a source; if a sentence needs a fact you don't have, write a simpler sentence.
- **Gurbani, and every Gurmukhi heading or label.** Leave them exactly as they are. Never letter-space or split Gurmukhi.
- **Honorifics.** Guru Sahib Ji / Guru Ji, never "the Guru"; full names with Ji; they/them, never he, for Guru Sahiban. "Sri Dasam Guru Granth Sahib Ji", never "Sri Dasam Granth Sahib Ji".
- **Quoted words.** A quotation attributed to someone (the charity's own words on About, Ustad Ji's wording) stays as it is.
- **Privacy and Safeguarding.** Same facts and the same commitments, in the same order; only the sentences get clearer.
- **The markup.** Only text between tags changes, plus `alt` text where it can be clearer. Class names, ids, `data-*` attributes, links, scripts and styles stay.
